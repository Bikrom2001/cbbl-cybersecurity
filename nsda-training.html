Competency-based programmes, assessed on what you can do

NSDA-aligned programmes are structured around competency standards rather than session counts. Each level states what a learner must be able to do, and assessment confirms whether they can do it.

Assessment

What "competent" means here

  1. Formative: lab tasks reviewed by the instructor after each module.
  2. Summative: a practical task against an observation checklist, plus a knowledge component where required.
  3. Evidence record: the assessor documents what was observed against each criterion.
  4. Result: competent or not yet competent — with a written gap list and one re-assessment opportunity.
  5. Moderation: a second assessor reviews a sample for consistency.

Certification

On successful assessment you receive a CBBL Statement of Competency listing the units achieved and the assessment date, and a CBBL Certificate of Completion.

We do not state or imply that a CBBL certificate is an NSDA certificate unless that is formally granted and documented.
